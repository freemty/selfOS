# Academic Style Rules

Source: Strunk & White, Orwell, Pinker, and Gopen & Swan. Adapted under CC BY 4.0 from [agent-style](https://github.com/yzhao062/agent-style).

## Canonical Rules

1. Define terms and acronyms; do not assume tacit knowledge.
2. Prefer active voice when the agent matters.
3. Use concrete, specific terms: “latency increased 40 ms,” not “performance degraded.”
4. Cut needless words such as “it is worth noting that.”
5. Avoid prefabricated metaphors and phrases.
6. Prefer everyday words over avoidable jargon: “use,” not “utilize.”
7. State affirmative claims affirmatively.
8. Match hedging to evidence: distinguish observation, belief, and inference.
9. Express coordinate ideas in parallel form.
10. Keep modifiers next to what they modify.
11. Put new or important information near the sentence end.
12. Split sentences over roughly 30 words when the split improves clarity; vary sentence length.

## LLM Failure Patterns

- Keep prose as prose unless the content is a genuine list.
- Do not use em/en dashes as routine sentence punctuation.
- Vary repetitive sentence openings.
- Remove filler transitions such as “Additionally” and “Moreover” when logic already connects the sentences.
- Do not close every paragraph with a redundant summary.
- Use one term consistently; do not redefine abbreviations mid-document.
- Use title case for English section headings unless the venue requires another style.
- Support factual claims with citations or concrete evidence.
- Prefer full forms over contractions in formal technical prose.

## Priority

Evidence fidelity and reader context are critical. Concreteness, concision, and claim calibration come next. Treat the remaining rules as defaults and use judgment when audience or venue conventions differ.
