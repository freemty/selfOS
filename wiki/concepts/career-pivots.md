# Career Pivots

## The PhD Pivot Problem

A PhD is 5-6 years. Research directions shift on 1-2 year cycles. This means most PhD students will face at least one major pivot. The question isn't whether to pivot, but how to pivot well.

## My Pivot: Theory to Systems

Started the PhD leaning toward theoretical ML — generalization bounds, optimization convergence proofs. By Year 2, I realized my best work was all systems:
- The custom Triton kernel I built for fun turned into a paper
- I kept volunteering for infrastructure tasks in the lab
- Theory seminars were interesting but I never lost track of time doing proofs

The Claude conversation that crystallized this decision (source: [[sources/cc-2026-01-20-why-i-chose-systems-over-theory]]) reframed the pivot: it wasn't "giving up" on depth, it was *following* my [[concepts/research-taste]] toward where I have genuine intuition.

## Framework: When to Pivot

### Signal 1: Energy Asymmetry
Where do you lose track of time? Not "what sounds prestigious" — what actually absorbs you. For me: writing CUDA > writing proofs.

### Signal 2: Taste Alignment
Can you sense what the "right" next step is in this area? If you can feel the bottleneck intuitively, that's taste. If you're always following others' framing, you don't have taste here yet.

### Signal 3: Output Quality Gap
Where does your best work come from? [[entities/alex-advisor]] noticed my systems papers were sharper than my theory papers before I did.

## How to Pivot Well

1. **Don't burn bridges** — theory background makes me a better systems researcher
2. **Build a beachhead** — my first systems paper gave me credibility before the full pivot
3. **Find allies** — [[entities/study-group]]'s systems track gave me a community
4. **Reframe the narrative** — "I chose the bottleneck that matters" > "I switched because theory was hard"

## Connection to [[concepts/research-taste]]

A pivot is itself a taste decision. Recognizing that the field's bottleneck is moving from algorithms to infrastructure (source: [[sources/notion-2025-09-15-research-taste-is-underrated]]) and positioning accordingly is exactly what taste looks like in practice.

## What I'd Tell Year-1 Me

Follow the energy, not the prestige gradient. The best researchers I know ([[entities/tri-dao]] is the clearest example) work on what genuinely excites them, and the prestige follows. The reverse almost never works.
