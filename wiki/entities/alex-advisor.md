# Prof. Sarah Lin (Advisor)

## Who

Associate Professor in CS, specializing in efficient ML systems and hardware-software co-design. My PhD advisor since Fall 2024.

## Advising Style

### Strengths

- **Asks the hard question first**: "Is this the right problem?" before "Is this the right solution?" This was a formative moment for my thinking about [[concepts/research-taste]] (source: [[sources/notion-2025-09-15-research-taste-is-underrated]])
- **Gives space for exploration**: let me spend a semester exploring theory before guiding me back toward systems
- **Reads the output, not the effort**: noticed my systems papers were sharper than my theory papers before I did
- **Connects students to the community**: introduced me to systems researchers at conferences

### Growth Areas

- Sometimes too hands-off when I'm clearly stuck
- Prefers papers with strong empirical results, which biases toward incremental systems work
- Office hours often double-booked

## Key Moments

### The "Right Problem" Conversation (2025-09)

I presented a custom CUDA kernel that achieved 1.3x speedup on a niche operation. She asked: "Who will use this? What does it unlock?" I couldn't answer. That exchange planted the seed for my thinking about [[concepts/research-taste]] — technical impressiveness is not the same as impact.

### Supporting the Pivot (2026-01)

When I told her I wanted to shift my thesis from theory to systems (source: [[sources/cc-2026-01-20-why-i-chose-systems-over-theory]]), she said: "I've been waiting for you to figure that out." She'd seen it in my work patterns for months.

## What I'm Learning From Her

1. Problem selection > solution elegance
2. "Your best thinking happens when your hands are on the keyboard" — her version of [[concepts/learning-by-building]]
3. A good advisor mirrors what you can't see in yourself
