# Research Taste Is Underrated

## Context

Written after a lab meeting where [[entities/alex-advisor]] pushed back on a project proposal. I spent two weeks building a custom CUDA kernel for a marginal speedup, and she asked: "But is this the *right* problem?" That question hit hard.

## Core Argument

Technical skill is table stakes. Every PhD student at a top program can implement a transformer from scratch. What separates impactful researchers from merely productive ones is **taste** — the ability to sense which problems will matter in 2-3 years.

I keep noticing this pattern:
- [[entities/tri-dao]] didn't just optimize attention — he picked the *exact* bottleneck that would unlock practical long-context models
- The FlashAttention paper isn't technically the hardest systems paper, but its problem selection was perfect

## What Taste Looks Like

1. **Sensing the bottleneck** — knowing where the field is actually stuck, not where it *says* it's stuck
2. **Timing** — working on something 6-12 months before it becomes obvious
3. **Taste in formulation** — finding the clean mathematical frame that makes a messy problem tractable

## Personal Reflection

I think my taste is improving. The [[concepts/learning-by-building]] approach helps — building FlashAttention from scratch gave me intuitions that reading 50 papers wouldn't. But I still catch myself defaulting to "technically impressive" over "strategically important."

[[entities/study-group]] discussions help calibrate. When I pitch a project idea and get blank stares vs. genuine excitement, that's a signal.

## Connection to Career

This connects to [[concepts/career-pivots]] — choosing systems over theory was itself a taste decision. I'm betting that the bottleneck is moving from algorithms to infrastructure.
