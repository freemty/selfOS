# Why I Chose Systems Over Theory

## Context

A Claude conversation processing a difficult decision. I had been wavering between pure ML theory (generalization bounds, optimization theory) and ML systems (efficient inference, training infrastructure). [[entities/alex-advisor]] gave me space to choose but I could tell she had a preference.

## The Conversation Arc

**Round 1: Framing the dilemma**
I described the tension: theory feels more "prestigious" in academia, but systems work has more immediate impact. Claude asked me to think about what I actually *enjoy* doing.

**Round 2: The honesty moment**
I admitted that my best days in the PhD were all building things — the Triton kernel, the custom data loader, the distributed training setup. The theory seminars were intellectually stimulating but I never lost track of time doing proofs the way I do writing CUDA.

**Round 3: The taste argument**
Claude helped me see the connection to [[concepts/research-taste]]: choosing systems isn't "giving up" on intellectual depth. [[entities/tri-dao]]'s work is as intellectually deep as any theory paper — it just expresses that depth through hardware-aware algorithm design rather than theorem-proof chains.

**Round 4: What this means practically**
Decided to:
1. Make my thesis centered on efficient inference systems
2. Audit the OS/architecture courses I skipped
3. Join [[entities/study-group]]'s systems reading track
4. Build, build, build — [[concepts/learning-by-building]] as core methodology

## Key Insight

> "The field's bottleneck is shifting from 'can we train it?' to 'can we serve it?' Your systems taste is actually your competitive advantage." — Claude's reframe

This connects to [[concepts/career-pivots]] — the decision wasn't really about systems vs. theory. It was about aligning my work with where I have genuine taste and energy.

## Aftermath

Two weeks later, I submitted my first systems paper (custom KV-cache compression). [[entities/alex-advisor]] said it was the most focused writing she'd seen from me. Confirmation that this was the right call.
